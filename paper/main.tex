\begin{abstract}
OpenAI's public 2026 mathematics release is an unusually rich public record:
mathematical manuscripts, source artifacts, Lean materials, and ten abridged
reasoning summaries. It is not a release of model weights, activations, full
prompts, or complete internal reasoning. We present a reproducible,
preregistered artifact audit at a pinned release commit. We find
\TotalFamilies{} result families and \TotalManuscripts{} catalogue-linked
manuscripts, with every linked PDF and TeX-source directory present in the
Git tree. We identify \LeanFamilies{} families with a Lean scope document and
\TraceCount{} released abridged summaries. Catalogue-linked manuscript count
does not meaningfully differ by scope-document availability (Mann--Whitney
$p=\MannWhitneyP$; permutation $p=\PermutationP$; rank-biserial
$r=\RankBiserial$). In a separately specified exploratory extension, we
structurally index every versioned Git blob (\TreeBlobs{} blobs; \TreeGiB{} GiB)
by path, object identifier, size, and artifact role. In a Version~2
exploratory continuation, we further extract \VTwoProofNodes{} literal TeX
environments and \VTwoExplicitEdges{} within-document source references from
\VTwoTexFiles{} source files, while separately retaining \VTwoMotifRecords{}
rule-based motif candidates. These structures are not proof verification or
semantic annotation. A frozen, family-disjoint prediction experiment finds
that literal-reference graph features predict the lexical \emph{induction}
label with AUROC \VThreeFunctionalAUROC{}, versus
\VThreeMetadataAUROC{} for matched metadata; this is an association with an
extraction label, not a recovered mathematical strategy. Controlled model
organisms further show that distinct hidden producers with identical observable
laws cannot be identified (\VThreeEquivalenceAccuracy{} accuracy against 0.50
chance). We release code, provenance hashes, derived data, figures, and a
venue-neutral reproducibility bundle. No result identifies OpenAI's hidden
reasoning or neural mechanisms.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Reasoning traces expose intermediate claims, proof organization, and references
to verification. Yet a persuasive chain of thought is not automatically a
faithful account of the internal computation that produced an answer
\citep{turpin2023language,lanham2023measuring}. Mechanistic interpretability
requires internal access and causal intervention, as in work on sparse features
and circuit-level representations \citep{elhage2022toy,templeton2024scaling}.

OpenAI's October 2026 public mathematics repository contains manuscripts and
supporting artifacts produced by an unreleased internal model
\citep{openai2026math,openai2026announcement}. It is valuable for studying a
public record of model-assisted mathematics, but it should not be read as a
transparent window into neural computation. We provide a pinned inventory,
a preregistered artifact-level comparison, and descriptive measurements of
the published summaries. We then separately demonstrate a genuine causal
mechanistic-interpretability intervention in a toy model.

\section{Scientific context: mechanistic interpretability}

Mechanistic interpretability seeks explanations expressed in a model's actual
computations: features, states, edges, and algorithms that can be
interrogated and causally manipulated. The circuit perspective developed from
feature visualization and transformer-circuit analyses
\citep{olah2018building,olah2020zoom,elhage2021framework} is deliberately
stronger than a useful behavioral description. It asks whether a proposed
mechanism predicts the effects of intervention.

There are instructive examples in accessible models. Work on induction heads,
modular-addition grokking, indirect-object identification, and a GPT-2
greater-than capability combines structure, activations, and interventions to
propose circuit-level accounts
\citep{olsson2022induction,nanda2023grokking,wang2023ioi,hanna2023greaterthan}.
Those successes are neither a license to infer circuits from prose nor a
guarantee that a localized explanation is complete. They instead specify the
kind of evidence missing for an unreleased model.

Causal tools make the distinction concrete. Causal mediation, activation and
path patching, model editing, automated circuit discovery, and causal
scrubbing each test particular counterfactual claims
\citep{vig2020causal,meng2022rome,geiger2025causal,zhang2023patching,goldowskydill2023path,conmy2023acdc,chan2022causal}.
They also expose failure modes: an ablated computation can be compensated by
others, and a single intervention can overstate a component's explanatory
role \citep{mcgrath2023hydra,finlayson2021causal,bau2019neurons}.

Recent benchmarks sharpen the evaluation standard. KisMATH aligns extracted
reasoning graphs with interventions on mathematical tasks, while MIB compares
mechanistic localization methods against shared causal targets
\citep{saha2026kismath,mueller2025mib}. These works rule out a priority claim
for graph-informed mathematical interventions here. Instead, the Version~2
continuation asks whether public research-source structure can generate
family-held-out causal hypotheses in a separately specified inspectable model.

Feature-level approaches address the fact that individual neurons can mix
several concepts. Toy superposition models, dictionary learning, and sparse
autoencoders supply hypotheses about more useful feature bases
\citep{elhage2022toy,bricken2023monosemantic,cunningham2023sparse,templeton2024scaling}.
In parallel, chain-of-thought and process-supervision research clarifies why
visible reasoning can aid evaluation while remaining distinct from the latent
computation that determined an answer
\citep{wei2022cot,lyu2023faithful,turpin2023language,lanham2023measuring,lightman2023verify}.
Our simulation adopts the causal-intervention side of this program while
making no claim to have recovered an arithmetic circuit.

\section{Evidence boundary}

\section{Identifiability and evidential boundaries}

Our framework separates artifact reconstruction (Level A), validated strategy
reconstruction (Level B), held-out functional prediction (Level C), causal
validation in an inspectable model (Level D), and target-producer mechanism
identification (Level E). Only Level A is fully available from this release.
Version~3 tests a deliberately weaker Level-C proxy: whether literal
source-graph features predict an already-fixed lexical extraction label in
family-disjoint public documents. It cannot validate a proof strategy because
the labels have not received independent semantic adjudication.

This separation follows causal-abstraction theory and benchmark work requiring
intervention against known causal targets \citep{geiger2025causal,mueller2025mib}.
KisMATH demonstrates graph-aligned interventions in accessible models, but
uses reasoning graphs and open models rather than a selected publication archive
\citep{saha2026kismath}. Public mathematical prose is not a faithful readout of
hidden computation merely because it is coherent \citep{turpin2023language}.
Consequently, no arrow in Figure~\ref{fig:overview} is an arrow into the
unreleased OpenAI producer.

The release contains public outputs and supporting artifacts. It does not
release weights, activations, attention patterns, optimizer state, complete
prompts, training data, or complete hidden reasoning. Therefore, our strongest
permissible claims concern availability, organization, and surface-text
statistics. We cannot identify a circuit, feature, attention head, latent
representation, or causal sequence in the OpenAI model. Nor can a manuscript
or a formalization scope page establish the correctness of a mathematical
claim. This distinction matters because process supervision can make
intermediate steps useful targets without making prose a complete causal
explanation \citep{lightman2023verify}.

The title's implication for mechanistic interpretability is methodological.
The public record can motivate frozen benchmarks and hypotheses for inspectable
models, such as whether a proof segment is causally necessary for a conclusion.
It cannot answer that question without a model whose internal states can be
recorded and manipulated.

\section{Corpus and method}

\subsection{Frozen source}

We acquired the Apache-2.0 licensed \texttt{openai/math} repository at commit
\texttt{fd4aeeb2...0529eeeb}; the acquisition manifest records the full
40-character identifier. The manifest
records SHA-256 hashes of \texttt{README.md}, \texttt{CONTENTS.md}, and
\texttt{LICENSE}, plus a successful \texttt{git fsck}. Windows path limits
prevent materialization of a few extremely deep upstream paths in a normal
working tree. Rather than silently lose them, the pipeline reads immutable Git
blobs directly from the verified object database.

The pre-analysis contract is in \texttt{RESEARCH\_PROTOCOL.md} and
\texttt{PREREGISTRATION.md}. The unit is a result family for catalogue
measures and a manuscript for file availability. Families are not independent
model runs.

\subsection{Measurements}

We parse each numbered family and each linked PDF in \texttt{CONTENTS.md}. For
each manuscript, we measure presence of the linked PDF, a non-vendor TeX file,
and a README marker. For each family, we measure whether
\texttt{lean/docs/NNN.md} exists. This is Lean scope-document availability,
not verified correctness or successful compilation. For the ten summary PDFs
named in the upstream README, we extract pages, word-like tokens, and
transparent keyword counts. Failures remain in a warnings table.

The sole preregistered comparison asks whether catalogue-linked manuscript
count differs between families with and without a Lean scope document. We
report medians, a two-sided Mann--Whitney test, rank-biserial correlation, and
a two-sided seed-20261009 label-permutation calculation using 10,000 draws.
The latter is a reference calculation, not a claim of independent random
families.

\subsection{Exhaustive release-tree extension}

The catalogue measures do not by themselves cover every versioned artifact.
We therefore registered a separate exploratory protocol before executing a
structural census of every Git blob at the same immutable commit
(\texttt{FULL\_TREE\_INVENTORY\_PROTOCOL.md}). The implementation resolves
the pinned commit once, reads immutable Git objects rather than the working
tree, and records each blob's path, object identifier, byte size, top-level
directory, extension, deterministic path/extension-based artifact role, and
catalogue or released-summary relation. This is exhaustive at the release-tree
level: it includes uncatalogued preprint bundles, sources, configuration, and
release metadata rather than treating the catalogue as the denominator. It is
not a semantic reading of every file, a proof check, Lean compilation, or an
expansion to all research artifacts OpenAI may publish elsewhere.

\subsection{Version 2 exploratory source-structure extraction}

Before any Version~2 extraction, we froze a continuation protocol and a
separate inactive confirmatory plan. The extractor reads immutable Git blobs
for all preprint TeX sources. It records TeX theorem-, lemma-, proposition-,
corollary-, definition-, remark-, and proof-environment candidates with source
commit, blob identifier, document identifier, result-family relation, and line
span. Graph A retains only literal within-document TeX references. Graph B
contains fixed lexical rules for candidates such as contradiction, induction,
construction, reduction, invariant, case splitting, limiting argument, and
equivalence. Graph A is not an entailment graph; Graph B is not semantic or
human annotation.

This checkpoint deliberately does not create validated mathematical
counterfactuals or execute a new model intervention. Lean kernel verification
does not by itself certify informal semantic fidelity
\citep{ammanamanchi2026faults}; without a detected Lean toolchain and
independent semantic audit records, no Version~2 counterfactual is labelled
verified.

\subsection{Scope documents and family size}

Both groups have a median of one linked manuscript. The comparison is small
and non-significant ($U=16{,}150$, $p=\MannWhitneyP$,
$r=\RankBiserial$); the deterministic permutation calculation gives
$p=\PermutationP$. This does not demonstrate equality, causal independence,
mathematical quality, or Lean compilation success. It only gives no meaningful
evidence that this simple catalogue-size attribute separates families by
scope-document availability at this release.

\subsection{Released summaries}

The \TraceCount{} named summaries span \TraceMinPages{}--\TraceMaxPages{} PDF
pages and \TraceMinWords{}--\TraceMaxWords{} extracted word-like tokens
(Figure~\ref{fig:traces}). Their non-random selection and heterogeneity make
them unsuitable for estimating properties of all model reasoning. They can
support qualitative study of the published text but cannot recover omitted
deliberation or establish faithfulness to unobserved activations.

\subsection{Family-disjoint functional reconstruction proxy}

We froze a single exploratory prediction task before inspecting Version~3
outcomes. The target is the extractor's fixed lexical \emph{induction} label,
not a semantic strategy label. The unit is an extracted node; the held-out
unit is a result family, with each unlinked document kept as its own group.
The structural logistic model receives node kind, within-document position,
document size, and literal-reference in/out degree. It never receives source
text, the matching words, document identifiers, or the target label. Matched
metadata-only and degree-permuted-structure controls use the same split and
model capacity.

The held-out set contains 8,541 nodes from 80 groups, with a 5.5\% induction
label rate. The structural model obtains AUROC \VThreeFunctionalAUROC{},
versus \VThreeMetadataAUROC{} for metadata and 0.71 after degree permutation.
The structure-minus-metadata AUROC difference is 0.028 (group bootstrap 95\%
CI 0.010--0.045). Figure~\ref{fig:v3-functional} visualizes the fixed
comparison. This modest association survives these controls, but does not
separate reusable proof strategies from mathematical conventions, editing, or
source-selection effects. It is therefore not evidence for Level-B strategy
reconstruction.

\subsection{Controlled hidden-process identifiability}

To test whether an output-only reconstruction can identify an otherwise hidden
producer, we generated 1,440 synthetic artifacts from six known process
families, froze their hidden labels, and supplied the reconstruction classifier
only observable motif, reference-count, length, and node-kind fields. Overall
held-out process accuracy was \VThreeOrganismAccuracy{}. Crucially, two
different producers were defined with exactly the same observable law. Their
identity accuracy was \VThreeEquivalenceAccuracy{}, below the 0.50 chance
reference (Figure~\ref{fig:v3-identifiability}). The controlled negative
result establishes an equivalence class: distinct processes cannot be recovered
when the available observations are deliberately identical. It calibrates an
identifiability limitation; it says nothing about which process produced the
OpenAI corpus.

\section{Causal intervention in an inspectable surrogate}

To distinguish output analysis from a mechanistic experiment, we preregistered
the extension in \texttt{SIMULATION\_PROTOCOL.md} before running
\SurrogateSeedCount{} independently initialized
\SurrogateParameters-parameter, two-layer, three-head Transformers from
scratch on all 100 ordered pairs of addition modulo 10. Their frozen
vocabulary has digits, a plus token, and an equals token; every seed reached
\SurrogateAccuracy{} training accuracy. This deliberately small, in-sample
task is not a benchmark of mathematical reasoning or generalization.

For each clean prompt $(a,+,b,=)$, we corrupted the left addend with
$a+1 \pmod {10}$. At each layer and position, we replaced the corrupted
post-block residual vector with the clean vector and measured recovery of the
clean answer logit relative to clean and corrupted baselines. This is an
activation-patching intervention \citep{zhang2023patching,geiger2025causal}.
For prompt $i$, recovery is
\[
 R_i = \frac{\ell_y(\mathrm{patched},i)-\ell_y(\mathrm{corrupt},i)}
 {\ell_y(\mathrm{clean},i)-\ell_y(\mathrm{corrupt},i)}.
\]
Near-zero denominators are excluded under the fixed protocol. We average
$R_i$ within model and use the four initialization seeds as the uncertainty
unit, reporting two-sided $t$ intervals with three degrees of freedom.

Layer 1 left-addend patching recovered \LayerOneLeftRecovery{} of the
clean-logit gap (95\% CI \LayerOneLeftCI{}), compared with
\LayerOneOperatorRecovery{} (95\% CI \LayerOneOperatorCI{}) for the
contextual operator control. The preregistered paired difference is
\LayerOnePrimaryDifference{} (95\% CI \LayerOnePrimaryDifferenceCI{}).
Layer 2 equals-position patching recovered \LayerTwoEqualsRecovery{}
(95\% CI \LayerTwoEqualsCI{}; Figure~\ref{fig:surrogate}). The last result is
an outcome-position positive control because the unembedding reads that final
residual position; it is not evidence for a distributed arithmetic circuit.

The surrogate result demonstrates a repeatable causal-intervention procedure.
It does not establish a reusable arithmetic circuit, necessity or sufficiency
of any site, generalization beyond its training table, or any property of
OpenAI's model.

\section{Limitations and research agenda}

This audit concerns one release commit; catalogue labels are upstream metadata,
not independent adjudication. The exhaustive tree census covers every
versioned blob structurally, but it does not read every file semantically. We
did not compile the substantial upstream Lean project or assess mathematical
validity. PDF extraction is imperfect, although failures are recorded. The toy
Transformer was trained on its evaluation table, so its result is methodological
rather than evidence of abstract reasoning. Most importantly, public output
artifacts cannot identify mechanisms inside an unreleased model.

Version~3 remains limited. Its source-structure outputs meet corpus integrity
and provide a documented exploratory input for G2, but they have not been
independently semantically audited. The finite arithmetic cases give only a
partial G3 check; they are not Lean-checked. The family-disjoint label
prediction and model-organism equivalence control make G7 partial. G4--G6 are
blocked: no eligible open-weight model, behavioral-competence result, or new
causal intervention exists. CPU-only PyTorch, no cached open-weight model,
and no detected Lean toolchain are recorded infrastructure constraints, not
negative scientific findings.

A credible next study should choose an open-weight mathematical model, freeze
prompts and scoring, collect clean and controlled-corruption runs, and test
causal effects through activation patching, path patching, ablation, or feature
intervention across seeds and held-out tasks. It should explicitly separate a
visible rationale from both the answer and the latent state, and test proposed
circuits against multi-site or resampling interventions rather than treating a
single patching map as confirmation.

A useful shared next artifact would be a versioned causal-transfer benchmark:
a corpus-derived task taxonomy, fixed prompt and counterfactual generator,
formal or otherwise transparent scoring suite, model-and-seed matrix, and
intervention registry all frozen before outcomes are observed. It should retain
only candidate explanations that predict intervention effects across model
families and held-out task variants, and document licensing and training-data
contamination risks. Such a benchmark would enable task-specific comparisons
of sparse probes, sparse feature circuits, and feature dictionaries under
shared control and interpretability criteria
\citep{gurnee2023haystack,marks2024sparsefeature,makelov2024sae}. This is a
proposed research infrastructure, not a claim that the current audit has
identified an OpenAI circuit.

\section{Conclusion}

The pinned 2026 mathematics release is not a window into an unreleased model's
circuitry. It is, however, a durable public specification of a demanding
mathematical-output domain: \TotalFamilies{} result families,
\TotalManuscripts{} linked manuscripts, Lean scope materials, and
\TraceCount{} released summaries, now situated within an exhaustive
\TreeBlobs{}-blob release-tree census. This study releases a pinned, auditable
map of that domain together with a deliberately separate causal-intervention
template.

That division makes the work actionable. Future studies can preregister
behavioral and structural hypotheses from the public record; instantiate
matched prompts, perturbations, and scoring rules in inspectable open-weight
mathematical models; and retain only candidate explanations that predict
intervention effects across seeds, model families, and held-out task variants.
Such studies would not identify OpenAI's mechanism. They could establish the
independently valuable result that particular causal computational strategies
are sufficient and robust within the tested model population for behaviors the
release makes observable.

The contribution is therefore a reproducible bridge from public mathematical
artifacts to falsifiable research on source structure. The bounded predictive
gain is retained as an empirical result, while the controlled equivalence
failure prevents it from becoming a claim about the producer process.
Interventions in accessible models would still be needed for a mechanistic
claim. Keeping these evidence types separate makes the work cumulative rather
than promotional.

\section*{Reproducibility and ethics}

Derived outputs are regenerated by the analysis, surrogate, and Version~2
proof-extraction modules documented in the repository README. The source
archive excludes third-party corpus files. The full-tree census is specified in
the accompanying inventory protocol. This study makes no claim about
proprietary model internals, intent, or hidden reasoning. The complete research
repository is available at the project repository URL.
